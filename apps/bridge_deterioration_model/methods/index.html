Methods

This project predicts bridge deterioration in FHWA Region 10 (Washington, Oregon, Idaho, Alaska) using a HyperNetwork that generates per-bridge Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks from structural, environmental, and geographic attributes.

Data

  • FHWA National Bridge Inventory. Annual condition ratings plus structural attributes for every highway bridge in the United States (Region 10 subset, years 2020 to 2024 in this release).
  • State DOT overlays. WSDOT, ODOT, ITD, and AKDOT load-posting, maintenance-history, and inventory supplements.
  • NOAA. Mean annual temperature, annual precipitation, and an empirical freeze-thaw cycle count, interpolated from the nearest weather stations with inverse-distance weighting.
  • USGS Coastal Salinity Index. A coastal-exposure proxy combined with a computed great-circle distance to the nearest coastline.

Model

A HyperNetwork (a multi-layer perceptron over static per-bridge attributes) generates the weights of a small LSTM. The LSTM consumes the time series of component condition ratings and per-year environmental exposure and forecasts the next k years of deck, superstructure, and substructure condition. A monotonicity regulariser penalises predicted improvement without a documented repair.

Baselines: a first-order Markov chain with Laplace smoothing (per-cohort), and a faithful reproduction of Miao et al. 2023's plain LSTM.

Reproducibility

All code is open source on GitHub. See the project repository for training scripts, a Streamlit explorer for model diagnostics, and the builder that precomputes the JSON payloads this site serves. A preprint is in preparation.

Limitations

Condition ratings are inspector-subjective, and NBI records are annual and sparse. Forecasts shown on this site are grounded in held-out cross-validation for years one through three; years four through ten, where shown, are unvalidated autoregressive extrapolation from the recursive rollout -- every bridge page that shows a ten-year curve marks this split explicitly and explains why the far end of the curve likely understates long-run deterioration. Some bridges have no forecast at all: fewer than two years of recorded inspection history is not enough to fit either model, and those pages say so rather than showing a curve.
